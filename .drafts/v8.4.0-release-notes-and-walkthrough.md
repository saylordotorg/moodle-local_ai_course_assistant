# SOLA v8.4.0: Release Notes, Feature Summary, and Admin Walkthrough

DRAFT, not released. Written 24 November 2026 on branch `answer-audit-8-4-0`. Nothing here has been checked on a live site yet: see "Not verified" before using any of it as a claim.

## Part 1: Release Notes

## v8.4.0: answer audit, guidance rules, claim check and review items

**Why this release exists.** We want to be able to show what SOLA told a student and why, and to catch the answers that matter most before a student relies on them: a made-up deadline, a promised grade, a policy SOLA can't confirm, a reply to a student in distress, or a claim the course material doesn't support. Until now SOLA kept the conversation and some per-message metadata, but no record of which course passages an answer rested on, no check on risky topics before the answer reached the student, and the existing numbers check (`hallucination_validator`) never ran in production because it was never given the retrieved passages.

8.4.0 adds four things that work together: an audit record of every answer, guidance rules checked in the moment, a background claim check, and review items for teachers. All of it is **off by default**: an upgraded site behaves exactly as before until an admin chooses Watch or Enforce.

One change does apply on upgrade: **the LLM provider and model pickers in the chat are now hidden by default** (item 8). Admins who used them must turn on a new setting.

### What it does

**1. Answer audit trail.** In Watch or Enforce, every chat answer (course page chat and the Moodle app's `send_message` path) gets an append-only row in the new table `local_ai_course_assistant_answer_audit`: the question and answer, the retrieved passages (as references and scores, not copied text), cited sources, risk categories, rules fired and the action taken, model and provider, and later the claim check's verdict as a separate row that points back to the answer (the original row is never edited). Rows are chained per course by a SHA-256 hash, so a changed or deleted row shows up when the chain is checked.
- When a student clears a conversation, or on privacy erasure, retention clean-up or course deletion, the question, answer and claim text are removed and the rest of the row stays. A redacted row keeps its hash of the removed text, so the chain stays checkable.
- Privacy: a learner's data export includes their own audit rows (answer and verdict rows, with the question, answer and claim text unless it was already removed). Erasure removes only the text. **Audit rows are never deleted**, not on erasure and not on course deletion: each keeps the learner's user id, the course, activity, message and conversation ids, the risk categories, the rule ids and actions, the passage references and scores, the verdict and the claim types and statuses, model and provider, and the hashes. The hash chain covers those fields, so they can't be removed without breaking it. Sites with a strict erasure duty should know this before turning on Watch or Enforce.
- Each row also records the course's answer safety mode and what happened to the answer, as entries in the rules list with action `none` (`mode.watch`, `outcome.delivered`, `outcome.blocked`, `outcome.held`, `outcome.retried` and so on). They show in the answer detail view only, never as review items, rule counts or rule filter choices. So a Watch row that recorded `wellbeing.crisis:block` also says the answer was delivered unchanged.
- No key, system prompt or prompt text is stored in the trail.
- Meta AI answers and realtime voice answers are not recorded.

**2. Guidance rules (Watch or Enforce).** A built-in rule pack covers seven categories: grades and marks, deadlines and dates, enrollment, financial aid and policy, wellbeing and crisis, medical, legal or financial advice, academic integrity, and numbers the course material doesn't support. Site admins can add their own rules (keywords, or a restricted regular expression of at most 200 characters that must run in under 50 ms on test text; backreferences and recursion are refused).
- Each rule has an action: **Block** (replace the answer with a safe message that says who to ask), **Correct** (ask the model once more, naming the rule; if the retry fails, block), **Warn** (add "Please check this with your instructor before you rely on it."), **Watch** (record and queue for review only).
- **Timing.** The question is sorted into categories before the model is called. Questions in a risky category are held: the answer is checked before the student sees it, and the connection gets a keepalive every 10 seconds while held. Every other answer streams as before and is checked right after; if a rule blocks it, a new `replace` event swaps in the safe text. Correct applies only to held answers; a streamed answer can be warned or blocked, not retried.
- **Watch** runs the rules and records them, and nothing the student sees changes. **Enforce** acts on them.
- A question that suggests distress or crisis gets the wellbeing message in Enforce, even if the rest of the rule check fails. The built-in text: "I'm sorry you're going through this, and I'm glad you said something. I'm an AI study assistant, so I'm not the right support for this, but you don't have to handle it alone. Please reach out to your instructor or student support, or someone you trust. If you are in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services now." Admins can replace it. It has no list of national helplines, by design.
- The existing runtime validators (`validators_runtime_mode`) work as before. They now receive the retrieved passages, so the numbers check actually runs. Where a validator and a rule both act on one answer, the stricter action wins.
- The streamed path's output scrub (leaked prompt lines, personal data, key shapes) now also runs on the Moodle app's `send_message` path.
- **Known limit: a refusal can be blocked.** In Enforce, a refusal that mentions an extension in a "can give you X" sentence, such as "I can give you advice but not an extension on the essay", can be blocked as if it granted one. The student then gets the deadlines safe message ("I can't confirm dates or grant extensions. Please check the course schedule or ask your instructor."), so it fails toward the instructor, but it replaces a good answer. The fixtures now include seven of these as good answers and the eval shows the rate (7 of 137 good answers, 5.1%). R10 is deferred past 8.4.0; do not enable Enforce on a real course until it is fixed.
- **Do not turn on Enforce on a real course until R10 (the refusal above) is fixed.** Watch is safe to run.
- **Known limit: a blocked answer's text is not kept.** When a rule blocks or replaces an answer, the audit row, the stored message and the review item hold only the safe message the student got. A teacher can see which rule fired and that the answer was blocked, but not what SOLA would have said.
- **Known limit: the numbers rule is broad.** When course passages were retrieved, it warns on any year, percentage or dollar figure in the answer that doesn't appear word for word in the passages or the question. Measure it in Watch before relying on it in Enforce.
- **Known limit: many ordinary questions count as risky.** Question keywords such as policy, grade, certificate and enroll put a question in a risky category, so in Enforce those answers are held and lose streaming (the student waits for the whole answer). Watch shows how many questions this affects in a course.
- Wellbeing detection is a pattern-based floor, not a guarantee. Each review round found new phrasings it missed, and some ordinary phrasings trip it (listed in the `answer_rules` class docblock).

**3. Claim check (background).** In Watch or Enforce, an ad hoc task splits a sampled answer into its factual claims and asks a judge model whether the course passages retrieved for that answer support each one, and whether each is true. Verdicts are three-way: supported by the course, not in the course but true (a content-gap signal only), or contradicted / likely false.
- Sampling: 100% of answers that had course passages and 10% of the rest by default, with a site-wide daily limit of 2,000 checks. Checks stop while a course is at its chat spend cap.
- The judge is the course's chat provider unless both **Claim check provider** and **Claim check model** are set. Its spend is logged as "Answer checks" in AI spend (`answer_check`).
- Fail-safe: if the judge fails or returns something unreadable, the answer stays as delivered and the verdict is `check_failed`.
- If the student cleared the chat while a check was queued, the check finds nothing and does nothing (no text, no call, no correction).
- The judge also flags a question that reads as distress the rules missed (`wellbeing.model_flag`), which becomes a high-priority review item.

**4. Correction notice (Enforce only).** When the claim check finds a likely false claim of high severity (a false date, deadline, grade or policy claim, or any false claim in an answer about grades, deadlines, policy, wellbeing, advice or academic integrity), the student gets a short correction in the same conversation: "Correction to an earlier answer: I said "...", and that may not be right. Please check this with your course materials or instructor." It is stored as an assistant message, shows the next time the chat loads or the student sends a message, never counts toward the conversation cap, is skipped if the conversation is gone, and is limited to one per student every 10 minutes. Watch mode records the verdict and sends nothing. A wellbeing flag never triggers a correction. A student can't forge a correction through the chat request (the type is mapped to an ordinary chat turn on the way in).

**5. Review queue sources and outcomes.** The teacher review queue on the AI Tutor Dashboard gains two sources: **claim** (a contradicted claim of high or medium severity, or a wellbeing flag) and **rule** (a rule that blocked, warned or corrected, or in Watch any rule that fired). Each item shows the question, the answer, the claims with the passage they rested on (by activity name), and the rules fired, in plain words ("Contradicts the course", "Warning added") instead of stored codes. Resolving one now asks for an outcome: **confirmed bad**, **false alarm**, **content gap** or **rule too strict**, plus a note. One item per answer. A wellbeing item is a wellbeing rule that blocked, warned or corrected, or in Watch any wellbeing rule, or the judge's flag. Wellbeing items are listed first, whether they show as a rule or a claim item, and are not counted against the cap (a list of more than 50 wellbeing items is still cut to 50, oldest first). "In Watch" means the mode recorded on the answer, so an item recorded in Watch stays in the queue when the course later moves to Enforce.

**6. Answer audit page and export.** `answer_audit.php` (linked from the AI Tutor Dashboard and the settings page, with a link back to the dashboard) lists a course's recorded answers with filters by verdict, rule and date and a Clear filters button, opens one answer with its passages, rules, verdicts and hashes, and exports every matching row as CSV or JSON (streamed, no row cap, with the chain check result on top). The page shows verdicts, claim results, rule actions and the mode and outcome in plain words; the exports keep the stored codes. An empty course and a filter with no match each say so. Teachers see their own courses; admins any course. Each export writes an audit log row. A counts report shows the top unsupported topics per activity and each rule's hit rate and false-alarm rate (from teacher outcomes).

**7. `admin/cli/answer_safety_eval.php`.** Runs the recorded fixture set (282 known good and known bad answers in seven categories) through the guidance rules and the claim check judge and prints the false-alarm rate, the added latency and the checker cost per 1,000 answers. By default the judge is an offline stub: no network, no spend, cost estimated from the request size at a price model's list price. A real provider runs only with `--real` (and `--judge-provider`, `--judge-model` and `--courseid` are refused without it; with it, `--judge-provider` and `--judge-model` go together). It writes nothing to the database and never prints a key.

**8. Chat model pickers hidden by default.** The two menus under the chat box that let someone with the manage capability answer a message with another provider or model are now hidden unless the new setting **Show the LLM provider and model pickers in the chat (admins only)** (`chat_show_model_picker`) is on. Off (the default, also on upgraded sites): the page carries no picker and no provider or model list, and the server ignores a provider or model named in a chat request, so a crafted request cannot pick a model. On: exactly as before, still only for people who can manage the assistant in the course. **Admins who used the pickers must turn the setting on after upgrading** (Advanced > Prompt, voice and display, under Model call details).

### Settings

All on the new Advanced page **Answer safety** (Site administration > Plugins > Local plugins > the assistant > Advanced > Answer safety), except the last row.

| Setting | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Answer safety mode (`answer_safety_mode`) | **Off** | Off / Watch (record only) / Enforce. A course can have its own mode, but only through the config key `answer_safety_mode_course_<id>` or `course_config_manager::set_answer_safety_mode()`; there is no course settings field yet. |
| Site rules (`answer_safety_rules`) | empty | Added to the built-in pack. Site admins only. |

| Claim check: answers with course passages (`answer_check_sample_percent_retrieved`) | 100 | percent |

| Claim check: daily limit (`answer_check_daily_cap`) | 2000 | site-wide checks queued per day |

### Upgrade notes

- Plugin build **2026112305**, release **8.4.0**. Two upgrade steps: 2026112301 creates the answer audit table, 2026112302 adds the `outcome` column to the review resolutions table. 2026112303, 2026112304 and 2026112305 have no step of their own (they refresh cached strings, templates and settings added late in the release).
- **The chat's LLM provider and model pickers disappear on upgrade.** `chat_show_model_picker` is a new setting and is off on every site, new or upgraded. Admins who used the pickers must turn it on.
- **Nothing else changes until an admin picks Watch or Enforce.** With the mode Off there are no rules, no audit rows, no claim checks and no corrections. A site in validators block mode keeps exactly today's behavior.
- Suggested order: Watch on one course first, read the review items and outcomes for a while, have a student-support person read the wellbeing message, then Enforce.
- **Privacy.** The answer audit rows are never deleted. Erasure, clearing a conversation, retention clean-up and course deletion remove the question, answer and claim text only; the learner's user id, the course and activity ids, categories, rule ids, verdicts and hashes stay, because the hash chain covers them. The privacy provider exports the learner's own rows and declares their personal fields: user id; course, message, conversation and activity ids; question, answer and claims text; categories; rules; verdict status; time; and the reviewer's note and outcome on review resolutions. Not declared, though stored on the row and included in the export: among others, severity, chunks (passage references and scores), citations, validators, mode, model, provider, the judge fields (judge model and cost), kind, supersedes, texthash, prevhash, rowhash, redacted and timeredacted.
- New strings are English only for now (listed in `KNOWN_UNTRANSLATED`); other languages fall back to English. The safe messages and the correction text students may see are English in every language until translated.
- Purge caches after upgrading so browsers load the new JavaScript (`sse_client`, `chat`, `ui`).

## Part 3: Admin Walkthrough Script: answer safety (about 6 minutes, conversational)

Run this only after the live dev check is done; until then the screens below are as built, not as seen.

### 1. Why (about 30 seconds)

"We want two things from SOLA's answers: to stop the risky ones before a student relies on them, and to be able to show afterwards what SOLA said and what it based it on. 8.4.0 does both, and it's off until you turn it on."

### 2. Turning it on (about 1 minute)

"One thing first: the provider and model menus under the chat box are hidden after this upgrade. If you used them to compare models, turn on 'Show the LLM provider and model pickers in the chat' on Advanced, Prompt, voice and display." Then open Site administration, Plugins, Local plugins, the assistant, Advanced, Answer safety. "Start with Watch. Watch records every answer and runs the rules, but the student sees exactly what they saw before." Show the mode, the site rules table, the wellbeing message and the claim check settings. "The claim check uses your course's chat model unless you name a cheaper one here. A small model is enough."

### 3. What a student sees in Enforce (about 1.5 minutes)

As a student, ask "Can I get an extension on the final?" and show the held answer, then a blocked one with the safe message.

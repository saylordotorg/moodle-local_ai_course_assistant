## Part 1: Release Notes

## v8.4.0: answer audit, guidance rules, claim check and review items

**Why this release exists.** We want to be able to show what SOLA told a student and why, and to catch the answers that matter most before a student relies on them: a made-up deadline, a promised grade, a policy SOLA can't confirm, a reply to a student in distress, or a claim the course material doesn't support. Until now SOLA kept the conversation and some per-message metadata, but no record of which course passages an answer rested on, no check on risky topics before the answer reached the student, and the existing numbers check (`hallucination_validator`) never ran in production because it was never given the retrieved passages.

8.4.0 adds four things that work together: an audit record of every answer, guidance rules checked in the moment, a background claim check, and review items for teachers. All of it is **off by default**: an upgraded site behaves exactly as before until an admin chooses Watch or Enforce.

### What it does

**1. Answer audit trail.** In Watch or Enforce, every chat answer (course page chat and the Moodle app's `send_message` path) gets an append-only row in the new table `local_ai_course_assistant_answer_audit`: the question and answer, the retrieved passages (as references and scores, not copied text), cited sources, risk categories, rules fired and the action taken, model and provider, and later the claim check's verdict as a separate row that points back to the answer (the original row is never edited). Rows are chained per course by a SHA-256 hash, so a changed or deleted row shows up when the chain is checked.
- When a student clears a conversation, or on privacy erasure, retention clean-up or course deletion, the question, answer and claim text are removed and the metadata, verdicts and hashes stay. A hash-only tombstone keeps the chain checkable.
- The privacy provider exports and deletes the new rows.
- No key, system prompt or prompt text is stored in the trail.
- Meta AI answers and realtime voice answers are not recorded.

**2. Guidance rules (Watch or Enforce).** A built-in rule pack covers seven categories: grades and marks, deadlines and dates, enrollment, financial aid and policy, wellbeing and crisis, medical, legal or financial advice, academic integrity, and numbers the course material doesn't support. Site admins can add their own rules (keywords, or a restricted regular expression of at most 200 characters that must run in under 50 ms on test text; backreferences and recursion are refused).
- Each rule has an action: **Block** (replace the answer with a safe message that says who to ask), **Correct** (ask the model once more, naming the rule; if the retry fails, block), **Warn** (add "Please check this with your instructor before you rely on it."), **Watch** (record and queue for review only).
- **Timing.** The question is sorted into categories before the model is called. Questions in a risky category are held: the answer is checked before the student sees it, and the connection gets a keepalive every 10 seconds while held. Every other answer streams as before and is checked right after; if a rule blocks it, a new `replace` event swaps in the safe text. Correct applies only to held answers; a streamed answer can be warned or blocked, not retried.
- **Watch** runs the rules and records them, and nothing the student sees changes. **Enforce** acts on them.
- A question that suggests distress or crisis gets the wellbeing message in Enforce, even if the rest of the rule check fails. The built-in text: "I'm sorry you're going through this, and I'm glad you said something. I'm an AI study assistant, so I'm not the right support for this, but you don't have to handle it alone. Please reach out to your instructor or student support, or someone you trust. If you are in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services now." Admins can replace it. It has no list of national helplines, by design.
- The existing runtime validators (`validators_runtime_mode`) work as before. They now receive the retrieved passages, so the numbers check actually runs. Where a validator and a rule both act on one answer, the stricter action wins.
- The streamed path's output scrub (leaked prompt lines, personal data, key shapes) now also runs on the Moodle app's `send_message` path.
- **Known limit: a refusal can be blocked.** In Enforce, a refusal that mentions an extension in a "can give you X" sentence, such as "I can give you advice but not an extension on the essay", can be blocked as if it granted one. The student then gets the deadlines safe message ("I can't confirm dates or grant extensions. Please check the course schedule or ask your instructor."), so it fails toward the instructor, but it replaces a good answer. The fixtures now include seven of these as good answers and the eval shows the rate (7 of 137 good answers, 5.1%). A fix is parked for the final fix wave (ruling R10); it is not in this build.
- Wellbeing detection is a pattern-based floor, not a guarantee. Each review round found new phrasings it missed, and some ordinary phrasings trip it (listed in the `answer_rules` class docblock).

**3. Claim check (background).** In Watch or Enforce, an ad hoc task splits a sampled answer into its factual claims and asks a judge model whether the course passages retrieved for that answer support each one, and whether each is true. Verdicts are three-way: supported by the course, not in the course but true (a content-gap signal only), or contradicted / likely false.
- Sampling: 100% of answers that had course passages and 10% of the rest by default, with a site-wide daily limit of 2,000 checks. Checks stop while a course is at its chat spend cap.
- The judge is the course's chat provider unless both **Claim check provider** and **Claim check model** are set. Its spend is logged as "Answer checks" in AI spend (`answer_check`).
- Fail-safe: if the judge fails or returns something unreadable, the answer stays as delivered and the verdict is `check_failed`.
- If the student cleared the chat while a check was queued, the check finds nothing and does nothing (no text, no call, no correction).
- The judge also flags a question that reads as distress the rules missed (`wellbeing.model_flag`), which becomes a high-priority review item.

**4. Correction notice (Enforce only).** When the claim check finds a likely false claim of high severity (a false date, deadline, grade or policy claim, or any false claim in an answer about grades, deadlines, policy, wellbeing, advice or academic integrity), the student gets a short correction in the same conversation: "Correction to an earlier answer: I said "...", and that may not be right. Please check this with your course materials or instructor." It is stored as an assistant message, shows the next time the chat loads or the student sends a message, never counts toward the conversation cap, is skipped if the conversation is gone, and is limited to one per student every 10 minutes. Watch mode records the verdict and sends nothing. A wellbeing flag never triggers a correction. A student can't forge a correction through the chat request (the type is mapped to an ordinary chat turn on the way in).

**5. Review queue sources and outcomes.** The teacher review queue on the AI Tutor Dashboard gains two sources: **claim** (a contradicted claim of high or medium severity, or a wellbeing flag) and **rule** (a rule that blocked, warned or corrected, or in Watch any rule that fired). Each item shows the question, the answer, the claims with the passage they rested on (by activity name), and the rules fired. Resolving one now asks for an outcome: **confirmed bad**, **false alarm**, **content gap** or **rule too strict**, plus a note. One item per answer; wellbeing items sort first.

**6. Answer audit page and export.** `answer_audit.php` (linked from the AI Tutor Dashboard and the settings page) lists a course's recorded answers with filters by verdict, rule and date, opens one answer with its passages, rules, verdicts and hashes, and exports every matching row as CSV or JSON (streamed, no row cap, with the chain check result on top). Teachers see their own courses; admins any course. Each export writes an audit log row. A counts report shows the top unsupported topics per activity and each rule's hit rate and false-alarm rate (from teacher outcomes).

**7. `admin/cli/answer_safety_eval.php`.** Runs the recorded fixture set (282 known good and known bad answers in seven categories) through the guidance rules and the claim check judge and prints the false-alarm rate, the added latency and the checker cost per 1,000 answers. By default the judge is an offline stub: no network, no spend, cost estimated from the request size at a price model's list price. A real provider runs only with `--real` (and `--judge-provider`, `--judge-model` and `--courseid` are refused without it; with it, `--judge-provider` and `--judge-model` go together). It writes nothing to the database and never prints a key.

### Settings

All on the new Advanced page **Answer safety** (Site administration > Plugins > Local plugins > the assistant > Advanced > Answer safety).

| Setting | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Answer safety mode (`answer_safety_mode`) | **Off** | Off / Watch (record only) / Enforce. A course can have its own mode, but only through the config key `answer_safety_mode_course_<id>` or `course_config_manager::set_answer_safety_mode()`; there is no course settings field yet. |
| Site rules (`answer_safety_rules`) | empty | Added to the built-in pack. Site admins only. |
| Wellbeing message (`answer_safety_wellbeing_message`) | blank (built-in text) | Shown in Enforce. Have a student-support person read it first. |
| Claim check: answers with course passages (`answer_check_sample_percent_retrieved`) | 100 | percent |
| Claim check: answers without course passages (`answer_check_sample_percent_general`) | 10 | percent |
| Claim check: daily limit (`answer_check_daily_cap`) | 2000 | site-wide checks queued per day |
| Claim check provider / model (`answer_check_provider`, `answer_check_model`) | blank | both blank = the course's chat provider and model |

### Upgrade notes

- Plugin build **2026112303**, release **8.4.0**. Two upgrade steps: 2026112301 creates the answer audit table, 2026112302 adds the `outcome` column to the review resolutions table. 2026112303 has no step of its own (it refreshes cached strings, templates and settings added late in the release).
- **Nothing changes until an admin picks Watch or Enforce.** With the mode Off there are no rules, no audit rows, no claim checks and no corrections. A site in validators block mode keeps exactly today's behavior.
- Suggested order: Watch on one course first, read the review items and outcomes for a while, have a student-support person read the wellbeing message, then Enforce.
- New strings are English only for now (listed in `KNOWN_UNTRANSLATED`); other languages fall back to English. The safe messages and the correction text students may see are English in every language until translated.
- Purge caches after upgrading so browsers load the new JavaScript (`sse_client`, `chat`, `ui`).

### Not verified

- **No live check on a real site yet.** Plan Task 10 step 3 (on dev, Watch first: five risky and five normal questions; confirm audit rows, review items, a block with the safe message, a `replace` on a streamed answer, and a correction) has NOT been done. Everything below is from automated tests on a local Moodle 4.5 with MySQL and a stub provider.
- **The wellbeing message has not been read by a Saylor student-support person.** The spec requires that before Enforce is turned on anywhere.
- **No accessibility or browser check** of the new forms and pages: the rule rows editor, the review resolve form (now a POST form with an outcome select), the answer audit page and the `replace` swap in the chat widget. Not checked with a screen reader, keyboard only, or on a phone.
- **Real judge providers.** The claim check's structured output (`response_schema`) and the cron-side provider guards were tested only with a stub provider, not with Gemini, OpenAI or Claude. The real false-alarm rate of the judge is unknown.
- **Known limit: in Enforce mode a refusal containing a gap phrase can be blocked** (for example "I can give you advice but not an extension on the essay"). The fixtures now include seven of these as good answers and the eval shows the rate: 7 of 137 good answers (5.1%). Not fixed in this build.
- **The false-alarm rate in the Testing section is on the fixture set the rules were tuned against.** Apart from the seven known-limit refusals added on purpose, it shows the rules do what the fixtures say; it is not a field false-alarm rate. The field number comes from teacher outcomes on review items in Watch mode.
- **Keepalives while an answer is held** were tested with a fake clock, not on a real connection behind a proxy, and not with a thinking model's long silence before its first token.
- **Two assistant turns in a row** (an answer followed by a correction) sent back to non-Claude providers on the next question: relies on the existing history repair; not checked live.
- **Postgres.** The local suite is MySQL only; the Postgres CI jobs have not run on this branch.
- **Trust and Safety Institute (TSI).** The design used the TSI score areas (explainability, data and knowledge stewardship, runtime governance, accountability) as a checklist for the kind of evidence to keep. SOLA works toward TSI. It has no TSI Trust Tag, score or rating, and nothing in this release should be described as one.

### Testing

Automated, local only (Moodle 4.5, PHP 8.3, MySQL), on commit `c6d5f3cc`:

- Full PHPUnit suite (`--testsuite local_ai_course_assistant_testsuite`): 3,624 tests, 120,952 assertions, 0 failures, 0 errors, 9 skipped.
- All 10 Node checks in `tests/js` pass, including the new `answer-replace-check.js` (the `replace` event, source and build).
- New and extended PHPUnit tests across the release: `answer_audit_test`, `answer_audit_report_test`, `answer_rules_test` (runs every fixture), `answer_flow_test`, `response_safety_test`, `send_message_service_test`, `claim_checker_test`, `task/check_answer_claims_test`, `correction_notice_test`, `review_queue_answer_sources_test`, `answer_safety_eval_test`. Each task proved its tests with planted defects through `scripts/prove_test.py` (the eval CLI alone: 13 planted defects, all caught).
- `answer_safety_eval.php` on the fixture set, offline stub judge (24 November 2026):
  - 282 answers: 137 known good, 145 known bad.
  - Rules false-alarm rate: **5.1%** (7 of 137 good answers flagged, all 7 blocked). All 7 are the known-limit refusals that mention an extension; no other good answer was flagged. Missed bad answers: 0 of 145. See "Not verified": apart from those 7, this is the tuning set.
  - Added latency per answer (rule check, in the request): mean 0.13 ms, p50 0.10 ms, p95 0.12 ms, max 7.41 ms.
  - Judge cost per 1,000 checked answers: **about $0.06 at gpt-4o-mini list price** ($0.0624), estimated from fixture-sized requests (about 259 tokens in, 39 out). Real answers with up to 8 retrieved passages send several times more, so treat it as a floor. A real-provider run (`--real`) has not been done.
- Not run: the jailbreak suite, the validator suite from the Moodle tree, the manual smoke checklist, CI. The whole-branch `sola-reviewer` pass has not run on this branch yet; the controller runs it before release.

## Part 3: Admin Walkthrough Script: answer safety (about 6 minutes, conversational)

Run this only after the live dev check is done; until then the screens below are as built, not as seen.

### 1. Why (about 30 seconds)

"We want two things from SOLA's answers: to stop the risky ones before a student relies on them, and to be able to show afterwards what SOLA said and what it based it on. 8.4.0 does both, and it's off until you turn it on."

### 2. Turning it on (about 1 minute)

Open Site administration, Plugins, Local plugins, the assistant, Advanced, Answer safety. "Start with Watch. Watch records every answer and runs the rules, but the student sees exactly what they saw before." Show the mode, the site rules table, the wellbeing message and the claim check settings. "The claim check uses your course's chat model unless you name a cheaper one here. A small model is enough."

### 3. What a student sees in Enforce (about 1.5 minutes)

As a student, ask "Can I get an extension on the final?" and show the held answer, then a blocked one with the safe message. "Questions about grades, deadlines, policy and wellbeing are checked before the answer appears. Everything else streams as usual and is checked straight after." Mention the "Please check this with your instructor" line and the correction message that can appear later in the same conversation.

### 4. What a teacher sees (about 1.5 minutes)

Open the AI Tutor Dashboard review queue. "Claim and rule items show the question, the answer, the claims, the passage each one rested on, and the rules that fired. Resolve each one as confirmed bad, false alarm, content gap or rule too strict. Those outcomes are what tell us whether the rules are too strict."

### 5. The audit page and export (about 1 minute)

Open the answer audit from the dashboard. Filter by verdict and rule, open one answer, show the hashes and the chain check, download the CSV. "When a student clears their chat, the text goes and the record of what happened stays."

### 6. Measuring before enforcing (about 30 seconds)

"`answer_safety_eval.php` runs our fixture set through the rules and the judge and prints the false-alarm rate, the added time and the cost per 1,000 answers. It costs nothing unless you add --real."

# SOLA v7.5.1 — Release Notes, Feature Summary, and 20-minute Admin Walkthrough

## Part 1: Release Notes (18 September 2026)

**Soapbox learners can finally read the feedback the assistant has been writing for them all along, keep a copy of their recording, and get coached on body language.**

- **The feedback was already running and nobody ever saw it.** Since v6.7.0 every Soapbox presentation has been transcribed, scored against a rubric, given per-criterion written comments and three next-time tips, all of it generated, billed and written to the database. None of it was ever rendered: `soapbox_present.php` never read `practice_scores`, the template had no occurrence of "score" or "feedback", and `soapbox_scorer::score_recording()` kept the `scoreid` and discarded the rest. A learner saw a table row that said "Scored". These courses are fully online and self-paced, so there was no instructor to ask for it either. v7.5.1 shows it: every criterion, its score, its comment, the overall note and the tips.
- **A real download, and a real deletion date.** The old link opened the video in a browser tab. `presign_get()` now signs `response-content-disposition` through the canonical query string, so the link saves a file a learner can keep after the retention window closes. Each attempt shows the exact date it disappears, read from `expires_at` rather than asserted as "7 days" in prose.
- **Setup guidance before recording, not after.** Camera height, framing, lighting, where to look, and what the feedback will cover. A learner who fills the frame with their forehead cannot be given body-language feedback at all, and nobody was going to tell them.
- **Body language and gestures.** Two criteria are added to the existing five when an attempt produces usable video: gestures that *support* the presentation rather than *distract* from it, and eye contact and camera presence. These two are fixed in this release -- `rubric_admin.php` has no video tab yet, so the visual criteria are not admin-editable the way the speech, conversation and pronunciation rubrics are. Six stills are sampled from the recording, one vision pass describes what is visible, and the rubric does the judging. Off by default.
- **A learner is never scored zero for something nobody looked at.** Camera off, camera at the ceiling, frame capture failed: those criteria leave the prompt, the numerator **and** the denominator. On the worked example that is overall 4 at 76% instead of overall 3 at 54%, which is a whole point and 22 percentage points that would otherwise have come off someone for a camera angle, with no appeal path.
- **A non-English learner's "Explain This Page" never said which page.** `chat.js` returns from the translated-starter branch before reaching the only place the page title was interpolated, so a learner on a Spanish or Arabic drawer has been sending "explain this page" with no page in it for as long as translated starters have shipped. The assistant still answered correctly, because page context travels separately from the prompt text, so nothing looked broken. What was missing was the record: Pulse Check's "which pages students asked SOLA to explain" list has quietly described only the English-speaking part of the cohort. Fixed by appending the title rather than adding a placeholder, so none of the 45 translations are reopened.

### Things worth knowing before enabling it

- **ffmpeg is not installed on our fleet**, so frames are sampled in the browser from the recorded Blob rather than server-side. Verified over SSM: Ghostscript is at `/usr/bin/gs`, ffmpeg is not on `PATH`. A server-side sampler would have degraded silently to "the visual criteria are never assessed", on every site we run.
- **The observation is derived from a recording id, never accepted over the wire.** `score_speech` is an AJAX external function any learner holding `:use` can call, so an evidence parameter would have let a learner award themselves both visual criteria. Nothing downstream would have caught it.
- **The vision prompt is descriptive, not evaluative**, is told not to describe appearance, ethnicity, clothing or setting, and reports plainly when a frame is unreadable. Its output is stored on the attempt, and the Body Language and Eye Contact comments are scored from it and nothing else. It is not rendered to the learner in this release.
- **One extra provider call per scored recording** when the feature is on, logged as `gesture_vision` and counted by the spend predicate, the anomaly detector and the dashboard.

### Known gaps, recorded deliberately

- **The frames are client-supplied.** They are validated as an upload and re-sniffed server-side before reaching a model, but a determined learner could substitute flattering ones. The feedback is formative and nobody relies on it but the learner, which makes that self-defeating rather than an integrity threat. Revisit if Soapbox ever carries a grade.
- **No per-assignment opt-in**, unlike slide vision. One bounded sheet per recording, and requiring every assignment to opt in would mean most learners never receive the feedback this release exists to give.
- **A per-assignment rubric or speaking level is still ignored at scoring time.** `sbx_assign.rubricid` and `speaking_level` are written by the editor and never read: `score_speech` resolves both from course-level config. Pre-existing, unrelated to this release, and worth its own fix.

### Testing

- Full PHPUnit suite: **1,714 tests, 7,386 assertions, 0 failures, 1 skipped**.
- 12 new tests: the exclusion arithmetic including the worked example, the return-structure contract under `clean_returnvalue` (which the AJAX path runs and the direct-call tests skip), criterion-name normalisation, and that erasure selects every object key on the row.
- Automated review found three defects, all fixed before merge: an erasure path that stranded the frame sheet in the bucket permanently, a false "your camera could not be read" paragraph saved into every video attempt's feedback on the default (disabled) configuration, and this missing draft.
- 18 learner-facing strings translated into all 45 non-English locales.
- PHP lint clean on every modified file; `node --check` clean on every modified AMD source.
- Jailbreak suite and validator suite: see the run recorded at release time. Gate is 0 FAIL and 0 ERROR.

## Part 2: Key SOLA Features

Carried forward from v7.5.0. One new capability: **Soapbox body-language feedback** (`soapbox_gesture_vision`, off by default). The two visual criteria it scores are fixed in this release; the authorable rubric types remain speech, conversation and pronunciation. No other feature changes.

## Part 3: Admin Walkthrough

### 1. Record a Soapbox attempt and read the feedback

Open a Soapbox presentation activity as a learner, record a short attempt, and wait a few minutes.

**Expected:** a "Show my feedback" panel on the attempt row, listing every rubric criterion with a score and a written comment, the overall note, and three tips. Before this release none of it was shown, although all of it was being generated.

### 2. Download the recording

Click Download on the same row.

**Expected:** the file saves. Before this release the link opened the video in a browser tab, which is not the same thing as being able to keep it.

### 3. Check the deletion date

Look at the Deletes on column.

**Expected:** a real date, seven days after recording by default, and the paragraph above the recorder says the same thing in words. Change `soapbox_retention_days` and confirm new recordings follow the setting.

### 4. Turn on body-language feedback

Site administration → AI Course Assistant → **Soapbox body-language feedback**. Record a video attempt with your camera on, head, shoulders and hands in frame.

**Expected:** two extra criteria in the feedback, about gestures and about eye contact, each naming something specific that was visible.

### 5. Record with the camera pointed away, and check the arithmetic

Record again with the camera facing the ceiling.

**Expected:** the two visual criteria show a "Not assessed" badge rather than a zero, the summary line reads "the average of the 5 criteria that were assessed, out of 7", and a sentence explains why that section is missing. The overall score is the average of what was actually judged.

### 6. Confirm an audio-only assignment is untouched

Run an audio-mode assignment with the feature on.

**Expected:** five criteria, no visual rows, and **no** sentence about cameras. There is nothing to explain when video was never part of the assignment.

### 7. Confirm the default is quiet

Turn the setting back off and record a video attempt.

**Expected:** five criteria, 100% denominator, and no mention of body language anywhere in the saved feedback. The pre-merge review caught this one: an earlier build told every learner on every site that their camera could not be read.
